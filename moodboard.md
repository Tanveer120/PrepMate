## 1. Brand Mood

### Emotional Atmosphere

PrepMate lives in the space between **a warm cup of tea** and **the satisfying click of a favorite pen opening**. It is not an app that demands attention — it *invites* presence. The emotional atmosphere should feel like settling into a well-loved study corner on a rainy afternoon: blanket nearby, desk lamp on, notebook open, and a quiet sense that everything is going to be okay.

The platform wraps exam preparation — an inherently stressful activity — in an environment that actively **reduces anxiety** and **increases comfort**. Every pixel should whisper: *"Take your time. You've got this."*

### Emotional Keywords

| Category | Keywords |
|---|---|
| **Warmth** | cozy, gentle, soft, tender, inviting, snug |
| **Calm** | peaceful, serene, unhurried, quiet, still, restful |
| **Playfulness** | cute, whimsical, cheerful, lighthearted, sweet |
| **Trust** | safe, reliable, honest, supportive, reassuring |
| **Craft** | handmade, textured, analog, tactile, artisanal |
| **Focus** | clear, centered, intentional, mindful, grounded |

### Intended User Feeling

When a student opens PrepMate, they should feel:

- **Relief** — "This doesn't look like every other overwhelming study tool."
- **Comfort** — "This feels like *my* space, not a corporate product."
- **Motivation** — "I actually *want* to do a few questions right now."
- **Ownership** — "This notebook is mine. My progress, my stickers, my notes."
- **Gentleness** — "Even when I get things wrong, it's okay here."

### Psychological Atmosphere

The psychological underpinning of PrepMate's mood is rooted in **environmental psychology** — the idea that your surroundings shape your mental state. Digital environments are no exception.

- **Low visual noise** reduces cognitive load, freeing working memory for actual studying.
- **Warm color temperatures** activate parasympathetic responses (calm, rest, digest).
- **Rounded shapes** are perceived as friendlier and less threatening than sharp angles.
- **Analog textures** (paper grain, watercolor) create a sense of tactile connection that pure digital flatness lacks.
- **A mascot companion** provides parasocial comfort — the feeling of "studying with a friend."

### Sensory Metaphors

PrepMate should evoke these multi-sensory experiences:

| Sense | Metaphor |
|---|---|
| **Touch** | The texture of thick watercolor paper under your fingers |
| **Sight** | Soft morning light filtering through a window onto an open notebook |
| **Sound** | Rain tapping gently on a window while you study |
| **Smell** | Freshly sharpened pencils and warm chamomile tea |
| **Taste** | A warm matcha latte with a tiny foam art bear |
| **Emotion** | The satisfaction of placing a sticker on a completed page |

## 2. Visual Inspiration Categories

### 2.1 UI Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Soft, rounded container elements that feel like paper cards floating on a desk
- Generous whitespace — never cramped, always breathing
- Layered depth through gentle shadows and overlapping elements
- Muted, warm backgrounds with paper-like texture
- Buttons that look like stickers or washi tape tabs
- Navigation that feels like flipping through a notebook

**Textures:** Subtle paper grain overlays, linen-like canvas backgrounds, soft noise filters
**Shapes:** Rounded rectangles (12–18px radius), organic/imperfect circles, torn paper edges
**Colors:** Cream, warm whites, pastel accents — never pure white (#FFF) backgrounds
**Composition:** Centered content cards with decorative margin elements (stickers, doodles)
**Emotional Feeling:** Like using a beautifully designed planner app, not a data dashboard

### 2.2 Watercolor Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Soft pigment bleeds with organic, irregular edges
- Semi-transparent washes layered to create depth
- Visible paper texture showing through thin paint layers
- Color mixing at boundaries (wet-on-wet effect)
- Intentional imperfection — no two edges are identical
- Subtle gradient blooms where colors meet

**Textures:** Wet paper absorption patterns, granulation in pigment, soft feathered edges
**Shapes:** Organic blobs, soft splashes, brush stroke shapes, irregular circles
**Colors:** Diluted pastels — soft blue washes, pale peach blooms, sage green pools, lavender mists
**Composition:** Used as backgrounds, dividers, accent decorations — never as hard borders
**Emotional Feeling:** The joy of watching watercolor paint bloom on wet paper — organic, alive, beautiful in its imperfection

### 2.3 Sticker Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Die-cut shapes with a subtle white border (kiss-cut sticker look)
- Slight shadow underneath to create a "lifted" paper effect
- Rounded, friendly shapes — stars, hearts, speech bubbles, small animals
- Flat or minimally shaded illustration style
- Slight rotation (1–5°) to feel casually placed, not rigidly aligned
- Small enough to be decorative, never overwhelming

**Textures:** Matte vinyl feel, subtle glossy highlight on some stickers
**Shapes:** Circles, rounded stars, speech bubbles, mini badges, fruit shapes, tiny animals
**Colors:** Slightly more saturated than the base palette — pop accents against soft backgrounds
**Composition:** Placed at corners, edges, and margins — like a student decorated their notebook
**Emotional Feeling:** The happiness of peeling a sticker sheet and placing them in your planner

### 2.4 Stationery Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Washi tape strips used as decorative dividers or header accents
- Paper clip and binder clip accents on cards or sections
- Pencil-drawn lines and doodle borders
- Sticky notes (Post-it style) as information containers
- Tab dividers peeking from edges (subject navigation)
- Stamp-like badges for achievements

**Textures:** Matte paper, translucent washi tape, metal clip sheen, eraser smudges
**Shapes:** Rectangular sticky notes, curved tape strips, round stamps, flag tabs
**Colors:** Stationery-grade pastels — the exact shades you find in Japanese stationery shops
**Composition:** Layered elements that feel arranged on a physical desk
**Emotional Feeling:** Walking into a Loft or Tokyu Hands stationery aisle — pure, organized joy

### 2.5 Notebook Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Ruled or dot-grid line patterns as subtle background textures
- Left margin lines (red or faded pink) as structural elements
- Ring-binder or spiral-bound edge accents on larger panels
- Page numbers in small handwritten-style typography
- Torn-edge page effects for cards that feel ripped from a notebook
- Fold lines and slight paper curl effects

**Textures:** Thick notebook paper grain, spiral wire texture, cardboard cover material
**Shapes:** A5/B5 proportions for content cards, rectangular with soft corners
**Colors:** Off-white / cream paper (#FFF9F6), faded blue ruled lines, red margin lines
**Composition:** Content presented as pages; navigation feels like turning pages
**Emotional Feeling:** Opening a brand-new Moleskine or Midori notebook — full of potential

### 2.6 Mascot Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Small, chibi-proportioned creature (large head, tiny body)
- Simple, expressive face — two dot eyes, small mouth, rosy cheeks
- Watercolor-rendered with soft edges (not hard vector lines)
- Holds study-related props: pencil, book, magnifying glass, cup of tea
- Appears in a consistent "companion spot" — never the center of attention
- 3–5 distinct emotional expressions (happy, thinking, celebrating, sleepy, encouraging)

**Textures:** Soft watercolor rendering with visible brush strokes
**Shapes:** Round, blobby, huggable proportions — inspired by Sumikko Gurashi, Molang, Rilakkuma
**Colors:** Warm, muted tones — cream body, pink cheeks, tiny colored accessories
**Composition:** Positioned in margins, corners, or speech-bubble areas — supportive, never blocking
**Emotional Feeling:** A tiny friend sitting on your desk who quietly cheers you on

### 2.7 Typography Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Handwritten-style display fonts for headers and decorative elements
- Clean, rounded sans-serif for body text (high readability)
- Occasional hand-lettered words as decorative accents ("Nice!", "Keep going!")
- Generous letter-spacing in headings for a relaxed, airy feel
- Ligatures and alternates in handwritten fonts for authenticity

**Textures:** Pencil-drawn letters, brush-pen strokes, ink variations
**Shapes:** Rounded letterforms (Nunito, Quicksand for body; Patrick Hand, Caveat for display)
**Colors:** Deep warm grays for body text (#4A4543), muted color accents for decorative text
**Composition:** Left-aligned body text, centered decorative headers, margin annotations
**Emotional Feeling:** Reading a friend's beautifully handwritten study notes

### 2.8 Illustration Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Simple, flat illustrations with watercolor texture applied
- Minimal line work — outlines are soft, sometimes absent
- Subject matter: study items (books, pens, lamps, cups), nature (leaves, flowers, clouds), food (tea, cookies)
- Small, decorative spot illustrations rather than large hero images
- Consistent 2–3 color palette per illustration
- Slight imperfection in shapes (hand-drawn feel)

**Textures:** Watercolor washes within simple shapes, pencil sketch under-drawing visible
**Shapes:** Organic, slightly irregular — nothing perfectly geometric
**Colors:** Match the PrepMate palette; never introduce jarring new colors
**Composition:** Scattered as decorative elements, not as primary content
**Emotional Feeling:** Flipping through a friend's illustrated study journal

### 2.9 Animation Inspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Slow, gentle movements — nothing snaps or jerks
- Floating, drifting quality — elements feel buoyant, not weighted
- Subtle parallax depth — foreground stickers move slightly differently than background
- Hand-drawn frame-by-frame feel for mascot animations (even if achieved via transforms)
- Watercolor "bloom" effects — color spreading organically
- Page-turning and paper-folding transitions

**Textures:** Smooth easing curves, organic motion paths (not perfectly linear)
**Shapes:** Animations preserve the rounded, soft shape language
**Colors:** Animations use the existing palette — no flash colors for attention
**Composition:** Motion guides the eye naturally; never distracts from content
**Emotional Feeling:** Watching a Studio Ghibli character perform a quiet, everyday action — making tea, opening a book, looking out a window

## 4. Color Mood Exploration

### The Psychology of Color in Study Environments

Color directly impacts cognitive performance, emotional state, and study duration. PrepMate's palette is designed with these psychological principles:

#### Warm Neutrals (Base Layer)
- **Cream / Off-White (#FFF9F6):** Reduces eye strain compared to pure white. Creates a "paper" feeling that grounds the interface in the physical world. Warm undertones activate comfort responses.
- **Warm Gray (#BFB6B0):** Provides necessary contrast for text without the harshness of cool grays. Feels like pencil graphite on paper.

#### Cool Pastels (Accent Layer)
- **Soft Blue (#7FB6FF / #E6F2FF):** Blue is associated with focus, clarity, and calm. Studies show blue environments improve creative thinking and sustained attention. Used as the primary accent — enough to guide action without overwhelming.
- **Sage Green (#C6EFD6):** Green reduces anxiety and signals safety/success. Used for positive feedback, achievements, and "correct" states. Evokes nature and growth.

#### Warm Pastels (Emotional Layer)
- **Peach / Blush (#FFD6D1):** Warm pinks activate nurturing, caring emotional responses. Used for highlights, stickers, and soft warnings. Feels like a gentle hug.
- **Lavender (#E8DCF5):** Associated with calm and spiritual restfulness. Used sparingly for special states (bookmarks, favorites).
- **Butter Yellow (#FFF3D6):** Warm yellows are associated with optimism and memory. Used for sticky notes, highlights, and "tip" elements.

#### Calming vs. Stimulating Balance

PrepMate maintains a **70/20/10 rule**:
- **70% calming neutrals** (cream, warm white, warm gray) — the paper and desk
- **20% cool pastels** (blue, sage) — structure, navigation, progress
- **10% warm accents** (peach, lavender, butter) — delight, reward, personality

This ratio ensures the interface never overstimulates while maintaining enough visual interest to prevent monotony during long study sessions.

#### Recommended Color Harmonies

| Combination | Use Case | Emotional Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Cream + Soft Blue + Peach | Default quiz view | Calm focus with gentle warmth |
| Cream + Sage + Butter | Success / results | Growth, optimism, achievement |
| Cream + Lavender + Soft Blue | Bookmarks / review | Reflective, personal, curated |
| Paper + Warm Gray + Peach | Settings / neutral UI | Grounded, trusted, simple |

## 5. Mascot Mood

### Personality Profile

**Name (working):** TBD — candidates: Mochi, Piku, Kumi, Nori, Bao

**Species Direction:** A small, round creature — hedgehog, otter, or cat variant. Must look like a plush toy brought to life. Not realistic, not overly stylized — somewhere between Sumikko Gurashi and a simple watercolor sketch.

**Core Personality Traits:**
- **Supportive** — never judgmental, always encouraging
- **Quietly enthusiastic** — celebrates wins with small gestures, not explosions
- **Sleepy-cozy** — resting state is a gentle, drowsy contentment
- **Curious** — occasionally reacts to what the user is doing with head tilts
- **Patient** — never rushes the user, never shows frustration

### Movement Style

All mascot movements should feel **floaty and weightless**, as if the mascot is a small watercolor drawing come to life:

- **Idle:** Gentle breathing (subtle scale pulse), occasional blink, tiny ear/tail twitch
- **Encouraging:** Small hop, tail wag, or a tiny raised paw
- **Celebrating:** Confetti-like sparkles around it, a bigger hop, rosy cheeks glow
- **Empathetic:** Slow head tilt, softer eyes, a tiny pat gesture
- **Sleeping:** When user is idle for extended time, mascot dozes off (closed eyes, small "zzz")

Movement is **never fast or jarring**. Peak velocity of any mascot animation should feel like a feather landing on a desk.

### Emotional Purpose

The mascot exists to:
1. **Humanize the interface** — transform a static tool into a companion experience
2. **Provide emotional feedback** — gentle reactions that acknowledge the user's effort
3. **Reduce isolation** — studying alone feels less lonely with a tiny friend
4. **Create attachment** — users return partly because they enjoy seeing their mascot

### Interaction Philosophy

- The mascot **reacts to the user** — it does not initiate demands
- Speech bubbles are **short** (3–8 words max): "Nice one!", "Take your time~", "You've got this!"
- The mascot **never blocks content** — it lives in margins, corners, or a dedicated small zone
- Interactions are **optional** — clicking the mascot might trigger a small animation but is never required
- The mascot **does not gamify** — it doesn't track streaks aggressively or shame missed days

### Animation Behavior Rules

| Trigger | Reaction | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Page load | Gentle wave or peek-in | 800ms |
| Correct answer | Small hop + sparkle | 600ms |
| Wrong answer | Sympathetic head tilt + encouraging bubble | 900ms |
| Bookmark added | Holds a tiny star, pleased expression | 700ms |
| Quiz complete | Celebration dance (restrained) | 1200ms |
| Idle (60s+) | Starts dozing off | Gradual over 3s |
| Return after absence | Happy wiggle + "Welcome back!" | 1000ms |

## 6. Environmental Inspiration

PrepMate should feel like a **place**, not just a tool. Each screen can be imagined as a physical environment.

### The Study Cafe

The **landing page** is a cozy study cafe. Warm lighting, wooden textures in the background, scattered books and stationery on a table. The user's subjects are displayed like menu items on a chalkboard or sticky notes pinned to a corkboard. There's ambient warmth — the kind of cafe where you want to stay for hours.

### The Rainy Evening Desk

The **quiz page** is a personal desk on a rainy evening. The desk lamp casts a warm pool of light on the question card (paper). Outside the pool of light, everything is softly dimmed. Rain taps on an imagined window. The focus is narrow, intimate, and deeply personal. There is nothing else in the world except this question and you.

### The Notebook Workspace

The **bookmarks and weak questions pages** are a notebook opened to a tabbed section. Bookmarks are sticky notes attached to pages. Weak questions are highlighted passages with gentle annotations. The feeling is of revisiting a well-organized study notebook — everything is where you left it.

### The Library Atmosphere

The **analytics page** is a quiet library reading room. Information is presented calmly in soft charts and gentle numbers. There is no urgency. The data is there to inform, not to pressure. Like looking at a library's reading log — interesting, reflective, never stressful.

### The Desk Lamp Warmth

Throughout the app, the **persistent warmth** of a desk lamp pervades. This is not the cold blue light of a monitor — it is the golden, directional warmth of a dedicated study lamp. Shadows are warm. Highlights are amber. The overall lighting model skews warm across every page.

## 7. Interaction Feel

Every interaction in PrepMate should have an emotional quality. This section describes what each core action should **feel like** to the user.

### Hovering

**Feels like:** Gently picking up a piece of paper from a desk — it lifts slightly, its shadow deepens, and you feel the potential of what's underneath. Hovering is an invitation, never a demand. Elements respond with a subtle rise (2–4px translateY), a softer shadow bloom, and perhaps a faint watercolor glow at the edges.

### Clicking / Tapping

**Feels like:** Pressing a sticker onto a page — a satisfying, tactile moment of commitment. There's a brief "press" (scale down to 0.97–0.98), then a gentle bounce back. The click should feel physical and complete, not floaty or uncertain.

### Answering Correctly

**Feels like:** The satisfaction of filling in the last blank in a crossword — a small wave of warmth. The correct option glows with a soft sage wash, the mascot does a tiny hop, and a subtle sparkle effect plays. The feeling is pride without arrogance — "well done" not "WINNER!"

### Answering Incorrectly

**Feels like:** A gentle "hmm" — not a buzzer, not a red flash, not shame. The incorrect option receives a very soft warm tint, a slight shake (not aggressive), and the mascot offers a kind head tilt. The explanation reveals gently. The feeling is: "Let's understand this together."

### Bookmarking

**Feels like:** Placing a favorite sticker on a notebook page — a small act of curation. The bookmark icon transitions with a flip or placement animation. A tiny sticker appears. There's a sense of "I'm saving this for later" — personal and intentional.

### Opening a Subject

**Feels like:** Opening a new chapter in a well-loved book. The subject card expands or transitions with a page-turn motion. The color scheme might subtly shift to match the subject's accent color. There's anticipation and readiness.

### Finishing a Quiz

**Feels like:** Closing a notebook after a productive study session — the gentle thud of pages coming together, a deep breath, and a quiet "well done." The results page appears with a soft watercolor wash reveal. Sticker badges float in gently. The mascot celebrates proportionally to performance — but always celebrates.

### Scrolling

**Feels like:** Running your hand along a long roll of paper — smooth, continuous, unbroken. No jarring layout shifts, no content jumps. Parallax effects (if any) are extremely subtle — like layers of paper at slightly different depths.

## 8. Anti-Patterns

### What PrepMate Must NEVER Feel Like

#### Corporate Dashboards
No dark sidebars with icon-heavy navigation. No data-dense tables with tiny text. No "enterprise" color schemes (navy, charcoal, corporate blue). No KPI cards with large numbers and tiny labels. PrepMate is a notebook, not a business intelligence tool.

#### Hacker / Cyberpunk UI
No monospace fonts as primary typography. No terminal-green-on-black aesthetics. No glitch effects, scanlines, or pixel art. No "matrix rain" or code-themed decorations. The audience is studying for exams, not infiltrating mainframes.

#### Crypto / Web3 Startup Aesthetic
No gradient meshes in neon purple and electric blue. No glossy 3D renders of abstract objects. No "dark mode with neon accents" as the primary theme. No aggressive CTAs ("MINT NOW", "STAKE YOUR PROGRESS"). No tokenomics-inspired progress systems.

#### Sterile EdTech Platforms
No generic blue-and-white interfaces. No stock photo humans pointing at whiteboards. No "Learning Management System" navigation patterns. No overwhelming sidebars with 15+ menu items. No progress bars that feel like project management tools.

#### Gamified Dopamine Machines
No flashing combo counters. No aggressive streak warnings ("You'll lose your streak!"). No competitive leaderboards (against others). No slot-machine reward mechanics. No loot box / gacha aesthetics. No XP bars that create anxiety about falling behind. PrepMate motivates through comfort and gentle ritual, never through fear of loss.

#### Overly Minimal / Brutalist
No completely flat designs with zero texture or personality. No stark black-on-white with no color. No intentionally "ugly" or "raw" aesthetics. PrepMate is minimal in *complexity*, but rich in *warmth* and *texture*.

#### Generic Material / iOS Clone
No out-of-the-box Material Design components without customization. No iOS-default styling. PrepMate has its own identity — it should be immediately recognizable as *PrepMate*, not as "a React app using a component library."
